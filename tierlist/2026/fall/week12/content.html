The Summer 2026 season came in with some seriously high expectations. Between major sequels like Mushoku Tensei: Jobless Reincarnation Season 3 (Mushoku Tensei III: Isekai Ittara Honki Dasu), Re:ZERO -Starting Life in Another World- Season 4 (Re:Zero kara Hajimeru Isekai Seikatsu 4th Season (Dakkan Hen)), and Ascendance of a Bookworm Season 3 (Honzuki no Gekokujou: Ryoushu no Youjo), plus highly anticipated projects like Jaadugar: A Witch in Mongolia (Tenmaku no Jaadugar) and The Ghost in the Shell (Koukaku Kidoutai: THE GHOST IN THE SHELL), this looked like one of those seasons that had almost everything going for it before it even started. And honestly, the variety was just as impressive — fantasy, isekai, school romance, slice of life, action, cats, monsters, villainesses, and plenty of other weird little corners of the seasonal catalog. On paper, this really did look like a loaded season.

What made the season memorable in the end, though, wasn’t necessarily just the obvious big names. If I had to pick the two shows that really defined Summer 2026 for me, Victoria of Many Faces (Tefuda ga Oome no Victoria) and Draw This, Then Die! (Kore Kaite Shine) would be right at the top of that conversation. They’re completely different kinds of shows, but both turned into genuine dark horses and gave me that special feeling of actually being excited to see what they would do every week — the kind of anime that reminds me why I keep watching this much stuff in the first place. Around them, You and I Are Polar Opposites Season 2 (Seihantai na Kimi to Boku 2nd Season) and Mushoku Tensei: Jobless Reincarnation Season 3 (Mushoku Tensei III: Isekai Ittara Honki Dasu) were about as dependable as expected, while Jaadugar: A Witch in Mongolia (Tenmaku no Jaadugar) delivered the kind of distinctive experience I was hoping for. Iron Wok Jan! (Tetsunabe no Jan!) also deserves a special mention for bringing back that wonderfully overheated ’90s-style energy with a production that clearly had a lot of love behind it.

There were plenty of other shows that made the season more fun in their own ways. Dara-san of Reiwa (Reiwa no Dara-san) was probably more of a personal hit than something that will work for everyone, but I absolutely had a great time with it. I also really enjoyed Hell Mode: The Hardcore Gamer Dominates in Another World with Garbage Balancing Season 2 (Hell Mode: Yarikomi-zuki no Gamer wa Haisettei no Isekai de Musou Suru 2nd Season), even if the entire cour felt much too rushed for what makes the series appealing. I understand there are probably all kinds of production and scheduling realities behind how much material had to be covered, but I still wish it had been given more room to breathe. Hell Mode isn’t just about battles, grinding, and leveling up — the quieter moments with family and the relationships built along the way are a huge part of its charm, and I wanted considerably more time with those elements. Even the battles themselves could have benefited from a slower, more deliberate treatment.

The Cat and the Dragon (Neko to Ryuu) was another one I quietly grew pretty fond of, while Saga of Tanya the Evil II (Youjo Senki II) ended up being much more enjoyable than I expected considering it normally isn’t exactly the kind of series I gravitate toward. And then there was Let’s Go Kaikigumi (Let's Go Kaikigumi) — another very personal favorite that simply added to the fun of the season. So while not everything lived up to the massive preseason expectations, there was still a surprisingly large group of shows I was genuinely happy to come back to every week.

Of course, the other side of Summer 2026 was considerably rougher. Re:ZERO -Starting Life in Another World- Season 4 (Re:Zero kara Hajimeru Isekai Seikatsu 4th Season (Dakkan Hen)) never quite reached the level I had hoped for, while Goodbye, Lara (Sayonara Lara) only strengthened my increasingly suspicious belief that anniversary projects are somehow cursed — seriously, what even happened there? Love Unseen Beneath the Clear Night Sky (Toumei na Yoru ni Kakeru Kimi to, Me ni Mienai Koi wo Shita.) initially made me think it might settle into a quiet, grounded romance centered on a blind heroine and patiently explore that relationship from an interesting angle, only to keep piling on more and more dramatic elements until the whole thing became increasingly difficult to take seriously. Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You (Super no Ura de Yani Suu Futari) also moved in the opposite direction from what I had hoped, gradually losing much of the appeal I found in its early episodes.

And then there were the real collapses. Ascendance of a Bookworm Season 3 (Honzuki no Gekokujou: Ryoushu no Youjo) was especially painful to watch fall so far after the change in production, while The World Is Dancing (World Is Dancing) left me wondering what it was ultimately trying to accomplish at all. A good number of the more disposable romantic comedies and isekai titles didn’t fare much better, and at least four shows that were still hanging around at the midseason point never even made it to the finish line. So Summer 2026 certainly had its disappointments and casualties along the way — but the shows that worked really worked, and thankfully there were quite a few of them.
