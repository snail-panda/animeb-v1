The Summer 2026 season came in with some seriously high expectations. Between major sequels like Mushoku Tensei: Jobless Reincarnation Season 3, Re:ZERO -Starting Life in Another World- Season 4, and Ascendance of a Bookworm Season 3, plus highly anticipated projects like Jaadugar: A Witch in Mongolia and The Ghost in the Shell, this looked like one of those seasons that had almost everything going for it before it even started. And honestly, the variety was just as impressive — fantasy, isekai, school romance, slice of life, action, cats, monsters, villainesses, and plenty of other weird little corners of the seasonal catalog. On paper, this really did look like a loaded season.

What made the season memorable in the end, though, wasn’t necessarily just the obvious big names. Victoria of Many Faces and Draw This, Then Die! became two of the biggest and happiest surprises of the entire cour for me. They’re completely different kinds of shows, but both had that special quality that made me genuinely look forward to seeing what they would do every week — the kind of anime that reminds me why I keep watching this much stuff in the first place. Around them, You and I Are Polar Opposites Season 2 and Mushoku Tensei: Jobless Reincarnation Season 3 were about as dependable as expected, while Jaadugar: A Witch in Mongolia delivered the kind of distinctive experience I was hoping for. Iron Wok Jan! also deserves a special mention for bringing back that wonderfully overheated, old-school energy with a production that clearly had a lot of love behind it.

There were plenty of other shows that made the season more fun in their own ways. Dara-san of Reiwa was probably more of a personal hit than a universal recommendation, but I absolutely had a great time with it. Hell Mode: The Hardcore Gamer Dominates in Another World with Garbage Balancing Season 2 ended far too quickly for my liking, but still did a solid job with what it had, while The Cat and the Dragon quietly became another one I grew pretty fond of. Even Saga of Tanya the Evil II, which normally isn’t exactly the sort of series I’d expect to click with this strongly, ended up being much more enjoyable than I anticipated. So while not everything lived up to the preseason hype, there was still no shortage of shows I was genuinely happy to come back to each week.

Of course, the other side of Summer 2026 was considerably rougher. Re:ZERO -Starting Life in Another World- Season 4 never quite reached the level I had hoped for, while Goodbye, Lara only strengthened my increasingly suspicious belief that anniversary projects are somehow cursed — seriously, what even happened there? Love Unseen Beneath the Clear Night Sky initially looked like it might settle into a quiet and surprisingly grounded romance built around its blind heroine, only to pile on more and more dramatic elements until the whole thing became increasingly difficult to take seriously. Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You also went in the opposite direction from what I had hoped, gradually losing much of the appeal I found in its early episodes.

And then there were the real collapses. Ascendance of a Bookworm Season 3 was especially painful to watch slide so far after the production change, while The World Is Dancing left me wondering what it was ultimately trying to accomplish at all. A good number of the more disposable romantic comedies and isekai titles didn’t fare much better, and at least four shows that were still hanging around at the midseason point never even made it to the finish line. So yes, Summer 2026 absolutely had its share of disappointments and dropouts — but with shows like Victoria of Many Faces, Draw This, Then Die!, You and I Are Polar Opposites, Mushoku Tensei, and Jaadugar carrying so much of the season, there was still more than enough here to make the ride worthwhile.
